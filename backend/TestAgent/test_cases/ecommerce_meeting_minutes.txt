MEETING NOTES:

Alex opened the meeting by sharing the vision for UrbanCart. He explained that we're building a multi-vendor marketplace similar to Etsy but focused on urban lifestyle products - handmade goods, local artisan products, vintage items, and sustainable products. The goal is to connect local sellers with conscious consumers.

KEY BUSINESS OBJECTIVES (from Alex's presentation):
- Launch MVP in 6 months (November 2026)
- Onboard 500 sellers in first 3 months post-launch
- Achieve $1M GMV (Gross Merchandise Value) in first year
- Take 15% commission on each sale (10% platform fee + 5% payment processing)
- Target market: Urban millennials and Gen Z (25-40 years old)
- Focus cities: San Francisco, Portland, Austin, Brooklyn, Seattle

Maria presented market research findings. She mentioned that Etsy has 7.5 million active sellers but is becoming too crowded. There's an opportunity for a curated, local-focused marketplace. She showed data that 73% of millennials prefer buying from small businesses and 68% are willing to pay more for sustainable products.

COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS (Maria's notes):
- Etsy: Too broad, not local-focused, high fees (6.5% + payment processing)
- Amazon Handmade: Lacks community feel, corporate image
- Facebook Marketplace: No curation, trust issues
- Local craft fairs: Limited reach, seasonal

Our differentiator: Curated local marketplace with strong community features and seller support.

James led the discussion on core features. There was a lot of back and forth on what should be in MVP vs Phase 2.

MUST-HAVE FEATURES FOR MVP (agreed after discussion):

Seller Side:
1. Seller registration with verification process (business license, tax ID)
2. Shop creation with customizable storefront
3. Product listing with multiple photos (up to 10 per product)
4. Inventory management
5. Order management dashboard
6. Shipping label generation (integration with ShipStation)
7. Sales analytics (basic - revenue, orders, top products)
8. Payout management (weekly payouts via Stripe Connect)
9. Messaging with customers
10. Review and rating system

Buyer Side:
1. Browse products by category and location
2. Search with filters (price, location, category, seller rating)
3. Product detail pages with seller info
4. Shopping cart
5. Guest checkout and registered user checkout
6. Multiple payment methods (credit card, PayPal, Apple Pay)
7. Order tracking
8. Review and rating system
9. Wishlist/favorites
10. Messaging with sellers
11. Order history

Platform/Admin:
1. Seller approval workflow
2. Content moderation tools
3. Dispute resolution system
4. Analytics dashboard
5. Commission tracking and reporting

Rachel showed some initial wireframes. Everyone loved the clean, modern design. She emphasized mobile-first approach since 65% of Etsy's traffic is mobile. She proposed using a card-based layout for product browsing.

Tom raised concerns about the timeline. He said 6 months is tight for a marketplace platform. Maria agreed but said we can use existing solutions for some components:
- Stripe Connect for payments and payouts
- ShipStation for shipping
- Algolia for search
- SendGrid for emails
- Twilio for SMS notifications

TECHNICAL STACK DISCUSSION:

Frontend (Lisa's proposal):
- React with Next.js for SEO benefits
- TypeScript for type safety
- Tailwind CSS for styling
- Redux for state management
- React Query for API calls

Backend (Tom's proposal):
- Node.js with Express
- PostgreSQL for main database
- Redis for caching and sessions
- Elasticsearch for product search (Tom suggested Algolia instead for faster implementation)
- AWS S3 for image storage
- CloudFront CDN for image delivery

Kevin talked about infrastructure:
- Deploy on AWS using ECS
- Use RDS for PostgreSQL
- ElastiCache for Redis
- CloudWatch for monitoring
- GitHub Actions for CI/CD

PAYMENT AND COMMISSION STRUCTURE:

This generated a lot of discussion. Alex wants 15% total commission:
- 10% platform fee to UrbanCart
- 5% payment processing (passed through from Stripe)

Maria pointed out that Stripe Connect charges 2.9% + 30¢, not 5%. After discussion, they agreed:
- 12% platform fee to UrbanCart
- Stripe fees (2.9% + 30¢) passed through to seller
- Sellers receive 85.1% of sale price (minus Stripe fees)

Tom asked about handling refunds and chargebacks. Sophie said we need a clear refund policy:
- Buyers can request refund within 14 days
- Sellers must respond within 48 hours
- If dispute, platform mediates
- Chargebacks handled by Stripe but seller is responsible

SELLER VERIFICATION PROCESS:

Rachel asked how we verify sellers. Alex said we want quality over quantity:
1. Application form with business details
2. Require business license or tax ID
3. Manual review by our team (Sophie will handle initially)
4. Phone verification
5. First 10 products must be approved before shop goes live
6. Ongoing monitoring for policy violations

Sophie mentioned this could be a bottleneck. Alex agreed to hire 2 more people for seller operations after launch.

SHIPPING AND FULFILLMENT:

Lisa asked about shipping. Tom said sellers handle their own shipping but we'll integrate with ShipStation to make it easier:
- Sellers can print shipping labels directly from platform
- Automatic tracking number updates
- Shipping cost calculator for buyers
- Support for USPS, UPS, FedEx

Emma (remote) asked about international shipping. Alex said Phase 2 - start with US only for MVP.

SEARCH AND DISCOVERY:

James emphasized that discovery is crucial for marketplace success. He proposed:
- Homepage with curated collections
- "Near You" section showing local sellers
- Category browsing
- Search with autocomplete
- Filters: price range, location (within X miles), seller rating, shipping options
- Sort by: relevance, price (low to high), newest, best selling

Tom suggested using Algolia for search instead of building our own with Elasticsearch. It's faster to implement and has great features out of the box. Maria agreed - saves us 3-4 weeks of development time.

TRUST AND SAFETY:

This was a big topic. Emma shared concerns from customer success perspective:
- How do we prevent fraud?
- What if seller doesn't ship?
- What about counterfeit products?
- How do we handle disputes?

Sophie proposed:
- Seller verification process (already discussed)
- Hold payment for 7 days after delivery before releasing to seller
- Buyer protection policy
- Review system with verified purchase badges
- Report listing feature
- Seller performance metrics (response time, shipping time, cancellation rate)
- Automatic suspension if metrics fall below threshold

Alex added that we should have a "Seller Standards" policy and enforce it strictly.

COMMUNITY FEATURES:

David (remote) suggested adding community features to differentiate from competitors:
- Seller stories/profiles
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Local events calendar
- Blog with seller spotlights
- Social media integration

James loved this idea but said it's Phase 2. Focus on core marketplace functionality first.

MOBILE APP:

Rachel asked if we need native mobile apps. Alex said not for MVP - responsive web is enough. Native apps in Phase 2 if we see strong mobile adoption.

TIMELINE DISCUSSION:

Maria presented a timeline:
- Month 1-2: Core infrastructure, authentication, basic seller/buyer flows
- Month 3-4: Product listing, search, shopping cart, checkout
- Month 5: Payment integration, order management, messaging
- Month 6: Testing, bug fixes, seller onboarding, soft launch

Tom pushed back again saying it's aggressive. After discussion, they agreed:
- Soft launch in 6 months with limited features
- Full launch in 8 months
- Phased rollout: invite-only beta (month 6), public beta (month 7), full launch (month 8)

BUDGET:

Sophie presented budget estimate:
- Development team: $600K (6 months, 8 people)
- Infrastructure: $50K (AWS, Stripe, Algolia, etc.)
- Design and UX: $80K
- Legal and compliance: $40K
- Marketing (pre-launch): $100K
- Contingency: $130K
- Total: $1M

Alex approved pending CFO review.

RISKS IDENTIFIED:

1. Tight timeline may lead to technical debt
2. Seller acquisition might be slower than expected
3. Payment processing issues could delay launch
4. Competition from established players
5. Regulatory compliance (sales tax, data privacy)
6. Scaling challenges if we get sudden traffic spike

MITIGATION STRATEGIES:

- Build with scalability in mind from day 1
- Start seller outreach 2 months before launch
- Use proven payment solution (Stripe Connect)
- Focus on differentiation (local, curated, community)
- Hire legal consultant for compliance
- Load testing before launch
