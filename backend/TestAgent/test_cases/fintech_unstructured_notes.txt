hey guys quick notes from the insurtech startup call today. these people are INTENSE lol

CLIENT: QuickClaim
DOMAIN: InsurTech / Health Insurance Claims
FUNDING: $5M Series A
FOUNDERS: 2 ex-insurance people + 1 tech person

THE PITCH:
they want to "fix health insurance claims" which is ambitious af. basically an app that helps people submit insurance claims faster and track them. they kept saying "we're the venmo of insurance claims" which doesn't really make sense but whatever

PROBLEM THEY'RE SOLVING:
- insurance claims take forever (average 30-45 days)
- people don't know how to submit claims properly
- lots of rejections due to missing info
- no transparency on claim status
- dealing with insurance companies is painful

their solution: mobile app that guides you through claim submission, uses OCR to scan receipts/bills, tracks claim status, and even predicts if claim will be approved

MAIN FEATURES (from what i understood):

USER SIDE:
- scan medical bills with phone camera
- OCR extracts all the info automatically
- app fills out claim forms for you
- submit directly to insurance company
- real-time claim tracking
- push notifications on status changes
- claim history
- estimate out-of-pocket costs
- find in-network providers
- prescription drug price comparison
- HSA/FSA balance tracking
- document storage (all your medical bills in one place)

they want AI to check if claim will be approved BEFORE you submit it. like a "claim score" or something. seems complicated but they're adamant about it

INSURANCE COMPANY SIDE:
wait they also want to sell to insurance companies??? this is confusing
- dashboard to manage claims
- fraud detection
- automated claim processing
- analytics on claim patterns
- integration with existing systems

so it's B2C AND B2B? need to clarify this

REVENUE MODEL:
they're not sure yet lol. options discussed:
1. charge users $5/month subscription
2. charge per claim ($2-3 per claim)
3. charge insurance companies for the platform
4. freemium model (basic free, premium $10/month)
5. take a cut of claim amount??? (this seems sketchy)

founder 1 wants subscription, founder 2 wants per-claim, founder 3 wants to charge insurance companies. they need to figure this out

TECHNICAL STUFF:

OCR/AI:
- use google cloud vision or AWS textract for OCR
- need to extract: patient name, provider name, date of service, procedure codes, diagnosis codes, amounts
- accuracy needs to be like 95%+ or people won't trust it
- they mentioned "AI claim prediction" but no details on how that would work

INTEGRATIONS:
- need to integrate with major insurance companies (UnitedHealth, Anthem, Aetna, Cigna, etc)
- each insurance company has different APIs (or no API at all)
- some use EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) which is old school
- might need to do fax integration (yes, FAX in 2026 lol)
- integrate with pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs)
- HSA/FSA account integration

COMPLIANCE:
this is where it gets scary
- HIPAA compliance (obviously)
- need to be a covered entity or business associate
- data encryption everywhere
- audit logs for everything
- patient consent management
- state-specific insurance regulations
- need to handle PHI (Protected Health Information)

founder kept saying "we'll just be HIPAA compliant" like it's easy. it's not easy

DATA STORAGE:
- need to store medical bills (images/PDFs)
- claim data
- user health information
- insurance policy details
- payment information

all of this is PHI so needs special handling

SECURITY:
- end-to-end encryption
- biometric authentication
- no screenshots allowed in app (for security)
- automatic logout after 5 minutes
- secure document deletion
- penetration testing
- SOC 2 compliance

TECH STACK IDEAS (my thoughts):

CHALLENGES I SEE:
1. HIPAA compliance is HARD and expensive
2. insurance company integrations are a nightmare
3. OCR accuracy on medical bills is tricky (handwriting, different formats)
4. claim prediction AI seems overpromised
5. revenue model is unclear
6. competing with existing solutions (Simplee, HealthJoy, etc)
7. insurance companies might not want to integrate
8. users might not trust uploading medical info to an app
9. what if OCR makes a mistake and claim gets rejected?
10. liability issues if their "prediction" is wrong
